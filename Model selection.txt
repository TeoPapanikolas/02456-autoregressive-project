Model Selection: LSTM vs. Transformer

We selected Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) as our main architecture for autoregressive protein sequence generation. Previous research has demonstrated that LSTM-based models can learn meaningful biological representations from amino acid sequences [1]. Moreover, Ebrahimi et al. [2] showed that recurrent neural networks can achieve better data efficiency than Transformers in certain sequence state-tracking tasks, suggesting a potential advantage of recurrent inductive biases in data-limited settings.

Although Transformers have demonstrated superior performance in large-scale protein language modeling [3], our training dataset contains approximately 16,000 protein sequences, and our computational resources are limited. Therefore, we chose LSTM as a practical architecture that supports sequential next-token prediction while keeping the model relatively simple and manageable. This choice does not imply that LSTM universally outperforms Transformers, but rather represents a reasonable balance between modeling capability, architectural complexity, and available resources.
